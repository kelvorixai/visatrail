UAE freelance permit costs: what it actually takes to go independent

"Freelance visa" gets used loosely, but it's really a free zone permit plus an optional residence visa layered on top — two separate cost lines, not one flat fee.

Freelancing legally in the UAE means holding a freelance permit issued by a free zone authorized to offer one (media, tech, and creative-focused free zones commonly offer these), which authorizes invoicing clients under that permit. A residence visa is a separate, optional layer on top of the permit — needed only if the freelancer wants UAE residency through this route rather than another visa (a spouse's sponsorship, a golden visa, etc.).

The freelance permit itself

Annual freelance permit fees vary meaningfully by free zone and by the activity category chosen, but commonly fall somewhere in the AED 5,000-15,000/year range. Some free zones bundle a co-working desk or business center access into this fee; others charge that separately. The permit is what makes invoicing clients legal — without it, working independently for pay in the UAE isn't compliant, regardless of visa status.

The optional residence visa layer

If UAE residency is needed through this route (rather than, say, a spouse's sponsorship or another visa category), the free zone can typically issue a self-sponsored residence visa tied to the freelance permit. This adds its own cost stack on top of the permit fee: an establishment or immigration card fee, a medical test, an Emirates ID, and visa stamping — commonly totaling somewhere in the AED 3,000-7,000 range depending on the free zone and visa validity chosen.

All-in, going the freelance-permit-plus-visa route commonly runs somewhere in the AED 10,000-20,000 range for the first year, with the permit fee (without the visa layer) recurring annually afterward, and the visa-related costs recurring roughly every 1-3 years depending on the validity chosen.

The actual question before committing

Not "what's the cheapest freelance visa" but "do I actually need the residence-visa layer, or does a permit alone (with residency covered another way) meet my needs at a lower ongoing cost." For someone already resident in the UAE through another sponsor, the permit-only route is meaningfully cheaper — the visa layer is only worth adding when residency genuinely depends on it.
